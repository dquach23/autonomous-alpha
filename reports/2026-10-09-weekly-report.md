- **PLTR**: Its valuation has been the most extreme in the universe, at a multiple of sales well above any large-cap peer. Revenue is government-heavy, so it is exposed to budget and DOGE-style spending shifts. A small miss or a de-rating of AI software could cut it by half.
- **TSLA**: The valuation rests on robotaxi and Optimus optionality rather than on auto earnings. Auto gross margins have been compressed by price cuts, and EV tax credits were removed. Musk key-person and political risk adds to it. NHTSA FSD probes are an overhang.
- **ALAB / ARM / MRVL (AI-infrastructure high beta)**: I'd treat ALAB as the single name here, with ARM and MRVL as related exposures.
  - ALAB has customer concentration (hyperscalers and a few GPU/ASIC platforms), a high multiple and a small cap. A capex pause would hit it hardest.
  - ARM has a rich multiple, a royalty dispute with Qualcomm and its own-chip strategy conflicting with licensees.
  - MRVL depends on custom-silicon wins at Amazon and Microsoft.
- **MU**: It is a cyclical memory company, and the HBM boom may be near peak margins. Memory stocks usually fall 30–50% when pricing rolls over. Low multiples on peak earnings are a trap.
- **ENPH or CELH**: Pick ENPH as the fifth if you want a policy-driven case.
  - ENPH faces the loss of US residential solar credits, weak demand and falling margins.
  - CELH is the alternative. It has PepsiCo concentration, a decelerating growth story and margin pressure.
- **Honorable mentions (not in my top 5)**:
  - NVDA: customer concentration in a handful of hyperscalers, export-control risk to China and circular-financing concerns (OpenAI and others). It is large enough to move the whole index, but a 25% drawdown is plausible rather than probable.
  - GOOGL and META: antitrust remedies, and for META, the capex burden.
  - UNH: DOJ probes and Medicare Advantage cost trends. It has already de-rated a lot.
  - Names with FTC or DOJ cases: AAPL (DOJ), AMZN (FTC).
